1. Automating Berylus: before and after

Operations · 2026

Before

  • Guest messages in the listing apps; cleaner scheduling in a group chat; maintenance in a notebook.
  • Every checkout and late-night question landed on me or a site supervisor.
  • Bookkeeping, tax prep, hiring paperwork and marketing content were separate roles I hired for.

After

  • Daily cleaner scheduling runs itself; guest messaging answers around the clock, with a person approving anything unusual.
  • Transactions categorized and tax schedules drafted from imports, for a professional to review; drafts, never filings.
  • 30+ hours a week saved per automated role across HR, sales, marketing, content, accounting and tax.
  • Berylus runs day to day on a part-time operations manager and the systems.

How it was verified

  • As a guest sees it: the listings kept a 100% response rate, replies inside an hour.
  • As an owner sees it: the operations manager can see every task and every exception on a record.
  • As the P&L sees it: the hours were counted by the roles I stopped hiring for.
  • Nothing sold before it survived my own properties first.

The tools became CoHostBnB, MyAIAccountant and TaxMeUp.

2. The agent runner: a governed build system, not a chat window

Systems · 2026

Anyone can ask a coding agent for a feature. What I built is the layer that lets one founder with no engineering team run a fleet of them at production scale without handing any of them the keys. The agents wrote the code; the design below is mine.

The governance is designed as a control system

I treat an agent the way I treated a contractor at Berylus. My brief carries the acceptance test, so done is defined before anything runs. The job leases a budget before it starts and releases it when it ends; once the pool is empty, nothing new launches. Each agent works under a short allow-list, and the runner refuses anything not on it instead of asking me, because "ask me every time" turns into "approve everything" by the second hour. Tests, a secret scan and bugclass-detectors gate every change. I make the release call. The runner appends every action, refusal and approval to a hash-chained record, so after an incident the log answers "what ran, was it allowed, who signed off" instead of my memory. The six controls are laid out on the systems page.

The scale is the point

Since April 2026 the system has completed more than 10,000 governed agent runs. Those runs built the 45 products on the companies page, their test suites and deployments, and they run the scheduled work that keeps the products up, on two servers I manage myself. Counted across every logged session since July, that is roughly 45 billion tokens of model context in more than 5,400 sessions. At that volume the controls stop being a preference; they are the only way the output stays reviewable by one person.

One agent pool, several lines of business

Several coordinating sessions, each responsible for one line of the business, dispatch agents through a single shared budget and one shared rulebook. A line that is idle lends its capacity to the one that is busy, and the budget of any job that dies is released on its own, so a stalled line can never block the others.

One system, every function

The same runner does more than build software. It runs Berylus day to day. It operates the security tooling: a secret scan on every push, a hook that masks credentials before they can reach an agent transcript, and bugclass-detectors inside review. It runs the content pipelines, where a rights gate refuses unlicensed footage and a person publishes each clip. And it ran my own job search, drafting only from a fact bank I approved, with every application reviewed before it went out. The control model, written up for a customer's agents, is public as governed-agents-case-study and sells as Elite Agentic Solutions.

3. Empire Security, and the bug classes behind it

Security · 2026

The parent

Empire Security is the security suite at Elite AI Holdings, launching in the fourth quarter of 2026. It puts under one roof the posture, evidence and visibility layer that a small company otherwise buys as a dozen separate subscriptions: Scan, Endpoint Posture, Cloud Posture, Email Guard, Evidence Pack, Repo Guard, Team Exposure, DAST, Logs and Detections, Backup Verify, Awareness, Vuln Intel, Security Home, Partners, an Agencies bundle, FleetFix, PrivacyKit, AuthGuard, EdgeWAF, EnvVault and UploadGuard. Five pieces are already live: bugclass-detectors, open source; Sentinel, uptime monitoring with hash-chained attestation; Scrubline, an exposure scan across data brokers; PhotoSafe, end-to-end encrypted photo backup; and Audit Ledger, hash-chained audit logs. Every module keeps the rule the rest of the catalog runs on: a person in front of anything irreversible, a record behind it. The suite began with one scanner, and that scanner began with a problem I kept meeting in review.

The problem

Reviewing what the agents shipped across 45 products, I kept finding one family of bug: the kind that leaves every dashboard green. Picture a background job that swallows the error on a database write, so its heartbeat stays healthy while rows go missing. Or a safety flag read from a setting that someone can repoint, so when the setting is absent the gate fails open. Or a secret captured once at start-up and never re-read after rotation. Fresh reviewers catch these line by line. Grep never does. The pattern lives in the structure of the code, not in its text.

What I decided

Treat them as classes, not instances. A fix that patches one swallowed write leaves the next one for tomorrow; a detector that reads the syntax tree finds all of them, in every file, on every change. I had been keeping a registry of bug classes since June 2026, logged from reviewing agent output; five of them were worth a public detector: hardcoded secrets, safety flags that fail open, secrets captured once and never re-read, swallowed write errors, and undefined names on rare paths. So I specified a parser-based scanner for those five, had the agents build it, and made it a gate: a high finding fails the build. Then I applied the rule I apply to every control: each bug class was planted in a test file, and the scanner had to catch it before I trusted it.

What it produced

Five bug classes caught at the syntax-tree level on every change, with tests and continuous integration on three Python versions, released under MIT as bugclass-detectors. It runs inside the agent runner's automated review, it became the BugClass product inside Empire Security, and it is what I point to when someone asks what reviewing AI-written code at volume requires.

4. CoHostBnB: fifteen years of guest messages, as a product

Product · 2026

The situation

I have answered guest messages since 2011, across my own listings and the units I managed for owners. The pattern that matters is simple: a wrong answer about check-in or a refund, sent mid-stay, costs more than a slow one. Most lodging software optimizes for speed and lets the model send. I wanted the speed without giving up the send.

What I decided

The model proposes, a person sends. Every guest reply is drafted by AI from the listing's own house rules and history, then a host or a team member approves it, edits it or writes their own. Housekeeping, maintenance and team handovers sit in the same workspace, from a single listing up to a hostel with dorm beds, so the person approving the message can see the room status behind it. Pricing is a real free tier (one listing, thirty AI drafts a month) with paid upgrades, and a managed co-hosting service for owners who would rather hand the whole thing over, priced as a share of booking revenue.

How it was governed

Nothing reaches a guest without a person behind it, by default and not as an option. Every draft, approval and edit is on a record, so an owner can see what was said and who said it. The same rule governs every product on the companies page that acts for a person.

What it produced

Berylus runs on it today, and the first live version shipped from the agent runner with the rest of the catalog in 2026. It is live at cohostbnb.xyz with free sign-up, and the design decisions are written up in cohostbnb-case-study. The same draft-then-approve shape runs Elite Support AI and MakeMyContent.

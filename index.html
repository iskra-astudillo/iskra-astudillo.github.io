Fifteen years running the company. Then I designed the machine that runs it.

The discipline that ran Berylus now governs a fleet of AI agents that build and operate a portfolio of AI products.

I founded Berylus LLC in 2011 and ran it as CEO, COO and CFO: property management, commercial leasing, short- and mid-term hospitality, a vehicle rental fleet and renovation projects, to more than $8M in total revenue. I sold the service to property owners and leased the same units to tenants and guests, so I have closed on both sides of the table the whole time, with a field team and subcontractors reporting to me.

I did not write the code. In 2025 I began directing AI coding agents on my own company's work, and in 2026 I designed the system that governs them: an agent runner on two servers I manage, where every job starts from my written brief, takes a budget, runs under an allow-list, passes review and waits for my release call. The agents write the code; the system design, the briefs and the decisions are mine. That machine automated nearly all of Berylus's daily work and turned the automation into the product catalog at Elite AI Holdings. Now I am looking for an employed leadership role where the full founder view is useful: operations, finance, people, and the automation layer that now sits under all three.

Selected decisions

Three calls I made as the owner of the P&L

Finance: growth paid for with structured debt, liquidity kept whole

Capital and financing · Berylus
Context
A property company grows by taking on units, and every lease, acquisition and renovation needs capital before it earns. The easy way to fund that is to drain the operating account, and the easy way to lose a year is to do it twice.
Decision
Finance growth through debt structuring and refinancing, with liquidity protected and the tax position planned together with the financing. Every project budget was built with how it would be paid for, a habit from starting my career as a loan officer refinancing residential mortgages. The same hand set the pricing and yield management on the units the financing bought.
Result
Fifteen years of full P&L ownership as CEO, COO and CFO, more than $8M in total revenue, with the cash position planned beside each financing decision rather than after it.

Portfolio: one catalog in parallel, one price ladder per buyer

Portfolio and pricing · Elite AI Holdings
Context
One founder, no engineering team, and a catalog of tools that Berylus needed and other operators would pay for. The usual advice was to pick one.
Decision
Build the whole catalog through one governed pipeline instead of one product at a time. Products that serve the same buyer sit under one company with one price ladder, so a customer sees a single pricing page. Every product launches on the free channels at once; paid spend and human time concentrate on three at a time. Free tiers only where the free output is public, a no-card 14-day trial elsewhere, and a managed service priced as a share of the customer's revenue so they pay only when the property earns.
Result
A full product catalog grouped under company brands in 2026, under one rule: wherever a product acts for a person, a person approves the action and a record sits behind it.

Governance: an allow-list and a hard budget instead of "ask me every time"

Control design · the agent runner
Context
Coding agents could produce the volume. An agent with a chat window and a credit card could not be let near production, money or a customer.
Decision
Treat agents the way I treated contractors: a written brief with the acceptance test in it, a budget leased before the job starts and released after, a short allow-list where anything not listed is refused, a person on anything irreversible, and an append-only hash-chained record of every action and refusal. Automate my own company first, before selling any of it.
Result
10,000+ governed runs since April 2026 built the products and now run Berylus day to day. The same control model, written for a customer's agents, became Elite Agentic Solutions. The six controls, on one page.

The six controls, on one page
  • Six controls, one recordWritten brief, acceptance test, budget lease, allow-list, automated review, my release call. Every action and every refusal on a hash-chained log.
  • One agent pool, several lines of businessSeveral coordinating sessions dispatch agents through one shared budget, so idle capacity flows to whichever line is busy.
  • One system, every functionThe same runner builds the products, runs Berylus, operates the security tooling and content pipelines, and ran my own job search from an approved fact bank.
  • Scale10,000+ governed runs since April 2026, and about 45 billion tokens of model context across more than 5,400 logged sessions since July, on two servers I manage myself.
